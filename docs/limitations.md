# Limitations and claim boundaries

This file is a required companion to any result generated by the repository. A successful pipeline run means the software completed under a stated policy; it does not mean the resulting dataset or model is publication-ready without review.

## Data coverage and provenance

- Public databases are incomplete, delayed, and corrected over time. Their absence of a measurement is not evidence of inactivity or safety.
- BindingDB exports can include records originating in ChEMBL, PubChem, articles, or patents. Source-label counts are not independent-evidence counts.
- Patents can report Markush examples, compound naming inconsistencies, or incomplete protocol context.
- PubChem assay rows are highly heterogeneous. Target, endpoint, unit, and readout must be established from assay metadata; unresolved assays are excluded by design.
- `document_year` is not necessarily synthesis date, assay date, or first public disclosure. ChEMBL/BindingDB commonly contribute publication or patent document years, while PubChem contributes the BioAssay deposit year. This mixed clock can create source-dependent temporal ordering even when date coverage is high.
- Raw files in the repository represent a snapshot, not a permanent mirror. When analysis is enabled, every result must cite the verified raw/processed/software/models/analysis/reports manifest DAG.

## Measurement comparability

- IC50, Ki, Kd, and EC50 answer different questions and must not be interpreted as exchangeable potency measurements.
- Assay-family labels are inferred from limited metadata and can remain unclassified or be wrong.
- Protocol variables such as protein construct, cofactor/partner, substrate concentration, incubation time, cell line, expression level, detection method, and laboratory may be missing.
- The default aggregate uses a median within endpoint/assay-family groups, but residual protocol heterogeneity remains.
- Within-structure disagreement can reflect experimental noise, different forms, target constructs, cellular context, or source errors. A median does not resolve the cause.
- Potential cross-source mirrors are linked only when normalized structure, endpoint, assay family, relation, and pActivity match exactly across sources. The fixed ChEMBL → BindingDB → PubChem preference is a metadata-based heuristic: it can miss mirrored records with rounding/context differences and can collapse genuinely independent equal-valued experiments. Same-source replicates remain and can still weight a median more heavily.
- Source errors and transcription errors can survive all automatic checks. High-impact rows require source-document review.

## Structures and identity

- Parent-fragment selection and neutralization can merge salt forms or formulations that matter experimentally.
- Tautomers are intentionally not canonicalized by default, so tautomeric representations may remain separate; enabling canonicalization could instead merge meaningful states.
- Incomplete or ambiguous stereochemistry in source SMILES cannot be recovered automatically.
- A standardized structure key is not a substitute for a lab compound/lot registration system.
- Mixtures, covalent species, metals, isotopologues, prodrugs, metabolites, and assay-generated species can require special handling.
- InChI generation and standardization can change across RDKit versions, which is why the policy and version are recorded.

## Units, relations, and exclusions

- Unknown or missing units are quarantined rather than guessed. This protects validity but can reduce coverage and introduce a documentation-quality selection bias.
- Exact-only ordinary modeling excludes censored-only evidence. This can bias the label distribution toward quantified compounds.
- Treating `<`/`>` thresholds as point values would be invalid; censoring-aware models have not yet been implemented.
- ChEMBL validity and duplicate flags are useful gates, not a guarantee that accepted records are correct.
- Default assay-variant exclusion avoids mixing mutants but may omit scientifically relevant evidence that deserves a separate task.

## Menin modeling

- The primary Menin result is scoped to `IC50 × biochemical_binding`; biochemical inhibition, cellular activity, Ki, Kd, EC50, and automatically enumerated tasks answer different questions and are exploratory unless separately pre-specified.
- Public Menin chemical space may be dominated by a small number of medicinal-chemistry series and patent families.
- A single scaffold holdout has split variance. Multiple pre-specified seeds or repeated group splits are needed to quantify this variance without using the final holdout for selection.
- Endpoint-specific tasks can have much smaller sample sizes than the pooled inventory. Models below the minimum support threshold should not be trained or reported as meaningful.
- Extra Trees and linear fingerprint models are baselines; a more complex model is not automatically more valid.
- Hyperparameter search is deliberately compact. This reduces overfitting opportunity but does not establish optimality.
- Model candidate holdout metrics are emitted for diagnosis. The final candidate must remain the one selected by training cross-validation.
- Mechanistic interpretability is limited. Fingerprint coefficients or tree importances are not causal substructure effects.

## hERG modeling and safety interpretation

- hERG public data pool different experimental formats, temperatures, species/cell systems, voltage protocols, exposure times, and endpoints.
- The headline classifier is limited to `IC50 × electrophysiology_functional`. This improves endpoint/readout specificity but does not make heterogeneous public electrophysiology protocols interchangeable. The pooled hERG model deliberately broadens endpoint and assay context and is sensitivity evidence only, never the primary safety estimate.
- The 10 µM blocker and 30 µM non-blocker boundaries are project communication thresholds, not regulatory or clinical QT thresholds.
- The ambiguous 10–30 µM band is excluded from binary training, which simplifies the task and changes its prevalence.
- Structures with conflicting blocker and non-blocker labels across retained endpoint/assay contexts are excluded from the binary classifier. This avoids arbitrary labels but removes difficult heterogeneous cases and can make performance optimistic for that subgroup.
- Platt calibration is internal. A calibrated probability on the same public-data distribution is not a clinical risk probability.
- The 0.30/0.70 low/medium/high bands are not optimized decision cutoffs.
- hERG inhibition alone is insufficient to infer arrhythmia risk, therapeutic index, exposure margin, or in-vivo safety.

## PK/ADMET inventory

- The PK/ADMET output is an observation inventory, not a set of trained endpoints.
- Rules based on endpoint names and description text can miss relevant data or misclassify context.
- Species, matrix, route, dose, formulation, and protocol differences prevent naïve pooling.
- Menin-associated ChEMBL molecule collection is not a systematic ADMET database search and can have strong publication bias.
- The chemical-intelligence stage uses PK/ADMET only as observation coverage and a data-gap flag. It does not treat coverage as favorable evidence or convert heterogeneous PK/ADMET rows into a desirability score.

## Chemical intelligence and prioritization

- The stage is restricted to the configured primary Menin endpoint/assay family. Its profiles, series, cliffs, and priorities do not generalize automatically to cellular, binding-constant, in-vivo, or other automatically enumerated tasks.
- QED, physicochemical windows, Lipinski/Veber counts, and apparent ligand efficiency/LLE are heuristic descriptors. Apparent efficiencies use IC50-derived pActivity rather than binding free energy and must not be presented as thermodynamic efficiencies.
- PAINS, Brenk, and NIH FilterCatalog matches are review alerts, not proof of assay interference, reactivity, toxicity, or compound invalidity. A clean alert panel is not proof of developability.
- Bemis–Murcko series and deterministic Butina clusters depend on standardization, fingerprint, and threshold choices. Acyclic exact-identity fallback is operational rather than a chemical-series theory.
- Achiral/chiral nearest-neighbor similarity and `local_novelty_achiral` describe only the analyzed public primary-task set. They do not establish worldwide chemical novelty, patentability, freedom to operate, or synthetic differentiation.
- Fingerprint cliffs can be representation or data artifacts. SALI can be unstable near unit similarity; shared assay/document context improves review priority but does not prove a causal SAR relationship.
- Single-cut matched molecular pairs are conservative descriptive pairs. Multiple pairs can share compounds and are not statistically independent; the reported transform is not a causal substituent effect.
- First-InChIKey-block connectivity groups can contain meaningful stereochemical, protonation, tautomeric, salt/form, and registration differences. They are an audit queue and must not be merged automatically.
- Observed primary hERG evidence overrides predictions. For unobserved compounds, only in-domain probabilities contribute safety desirability. Missing or out-of-domain evidence is `unknown`, receives no safety credit or complete-evidence score, and must never be described as low risk.
- Weighted scores, chemistry gates, tier thresholds, Pareto objectives, and sensitivity scenarios are configurable decision policies, not externally validated clinical or development criteria. Changing them after seeing results is outcome-guided selection.
- A build with no `priority_1_balanced_public_evidence` structures is scientifically possible and is not a software failure. It means the configured potency/chemistry/safety evidence requirements were not jointly met.
- The approved-reference panel is a dated public coverage benchmark for exact/related primary-task representation. It is not an efficacy, safety, superiority, or therapeutic-equivalence comparison. PubChem structures, FDA status, labels, and indications are time-sensitive and must be reverified for each release.
- The prospective-selection output is a diversity-constrained public-data experiment-design template. Its quotas and scaffold cap do not optimize experimental utility, and selection does not establish availability, identity, purity, synthesizeability, freedom to operate, efficacy, or safety. Until the frozen plan is executed and outcomes are independently assessed, it is not prospective validation.

## Splits, uncertainty, and applicability domain

- Scaffold and chemical-cluster splits depend on the selected representation and grouping algorithm.
- Acyclic structures use exact-structure groups in the scaffold strategy and do not receive a shared chemically meaningful scaffold.
- Automatic fallback to compound-grouped random splitting preserves duplicate control but weakens the intended validation claim; fallback results must be labeled.
- Bootstrap intervals resample whole scaffold groups when possible and quantify chemical-group sampling variability conditional on one trained model, split, and dataset. They do not include source, assay, curation, model-selection, or alternative-split uncertainty; a row-resampling fallback is weaker and must be disclosed from `resampling_unit`.
- The regression conformal interval uses out-of-fold residuals and assumes a form of exchangeability that scaffold/temporal shift can violate.
- The Tanimoto applicability-domain threshold is a heuristic based on training-neighbor similarity, not proof that an individual prediction is reliable.
- Similarity is representation-dependent. A structurally similar compound can still have an activity cliff.

## External validity

- There is not yet an independent external Menin test set with harmonized endpoints and protocols.
- Public hERG benchmarks require label and duplicate reconciliation before comparison; otherwise overlap can inflate performance.
- The generated public-data experiment template has not been executed as a prospective study; no newly proposed compounds have been confirmed by this pipeline through prospective synthesis/testing and blinded outcome analysis.
- Model usefulness should be evaluated against experimental decision outcomes, not only retrospective metrics.

## Confidentiality and release

- Public availability does not remove upstream license, attribution, or redistribution obligations.
- Proprietary inputs can leak through structures, identifiers, nearest neighbors, plots, aggregate counts, model files, caches, logs, or manifests.
- A model trained on internal data must be treated as internal unless the lab approves its release after disclosure-risk review.
- Combining internal and public data can make a derived table non-releasable even when its public portion is redistributable.
- Intake cohort roles do not by themselves enforce secure model partitions. Private orchestration and access controls must prevent `locked_external` and `prospective_blind` rows from affecting fitting, calibration, feature/threshold selection, curation-policy tuning, or stopping decisions; prospective labels must stay sealed until lock.
- Approved private `development`, `locked_external`, and `prospective_blind` cohorts require separate private build roots, manifests, and access controls. The public CLI is not a confidential-data ingestion route, and a combined private/public build must never replace the separately retained public and evaluation builds.

## Publication blockers

Generated artifacts can satisfy infrastructure checks without satisfying publication governance or external validity. At minimum, the following remain blockers until evidenced for a frozen release:

- a clean committed revision, exact environment, resolved configuration, passing tests/lint/quality gate, and valid six-manifest DAG when analysis is enabled;
- documented source-record and cross-source-mirror review, defensible task definitions, and disclosure of every split fallback and mixed temporal clock;
- data-owner authorization, confidentiality review, authorship/contributor decisions, repository/model licensing, and third-party redistribution review;
- independent external validation on compatible, de-duplicated endpoints; and
- prospective, preferably blinded, experimental validation after the model and analysis plan are locked.

## Minimum defensible claims today

With a verified build, this repository can support claims about:

- the count and characteristics of records included/excluded by a specified curation policy;
- internal cross-validated and holdout performance of disclosed baseline algorithms on that curated snapshot;
- calibration, interval, and similarity-domain behavior on the disclosed holdout; and
- data gaps requiring experimental follow-up.

It cannot by itself support claims that a compound is efficacious, selective, safe, clinically viable, superior to a standard of care, or ready for development.
