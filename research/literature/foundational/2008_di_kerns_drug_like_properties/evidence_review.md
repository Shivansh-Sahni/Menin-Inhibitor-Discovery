# Evidence review: Di and Kerns foundational chapters

## Source

Li Di and Edward H. Kerns, *Drug-like Properties: Concepts, Structure Design and
Methods: From ADME to Toxicity Optimization*, first-edition chapter extracts (2008):

- Chapter 3, *Barriers to Drug Exposure in Living Systems*;
- Chapter 16, *hERG Blocking*; and
- Chapter 19, *Pharmacokinetics*.

These chapters were supplied by Dr. Angelo Aguilar on 2026-07-22. They provide a useful
biological decomposition and historical medicinal-chemistry context. They are not current
primary evidence for numerical thresholds or modern hERG structural claims.

## Durable biological principles

- Exposure is generated by a sequence of interacting barriers: formulation/dissolution,
  pH and speciation, membranes, transporters, metabolism, plasma/tissue binding,
  distribution, and renal/biliary elimination.
- Barriers are dynamic and coupled. Efflux can recycle a molecule into compartments where
  metabolism acts again; high metabolism can prevent tissue exposure even when membrane
  permeability is favorable.
- PK summaries are downstream constructs. In particular, `CL = Dose/AUC_IV` and
  `%F = (AUC_PO/AUC_IV)(Dose_IV/Dose_PO)100`; using AUC together with its derived CL or F
  as independent labels would duplicate information.
- Vd is an apparent proportionality determined by blood/tissue unbound fractions and
  exchange, not a literal anatomical volume or proof of target-site exposure.
- hERG current block can depend on channel opening, central-cavity access, aromatic/cationic
  interactions, and trapping; observed risk also depends on unbound exposure.

## Historical claims that must not be promoted unchanged

- The chapter's `IC50/Cmax,unbound > 30` margin is a historical heuristic, not a universal
  safety guarantee. Assay protocol, state-dependent kinetics, other cardiac currents,
  exposure uncertainty, and clinical context remain necessary.
- The chapter predates the current cryo-EM structures and modern dynamic electrophysiology.
  Its generic basic-amine/lipophilicity pharmacophore is a baseline alert, not a binding
  mechanism for the present large molecules.
- Chapter 16 is internally inconsistent: its opening overview says hERG block may be
  reduced by *removing* oxygen H-bond acceptors, while its later modification table says
  to *add* oxygen H-bond acceptors. This contradiction is preserved and those directions
  are not encoded as scientific rules.
- Open-state preference and trapping are ligand- and protocol-dependent. Current primary
  literature includes examples where inactivation contributes, examples where it does not,
  and dynamic models that are not interchangeable with static conductance scaling.

## Project use

The chapters justify the architecture, not the final parameters: each barrier becomes a
state transition or competing flux, each measured assay becomes a protocol-conditioned
observation, and derived PK quantities become closure tests. Current structural and kinetic
primary literature supplies the receptor states, interactions, and falsification tests.

This distinction helps retain the professor's requested fundamental biology without
reproducing an older checklist of superficial property rules.
