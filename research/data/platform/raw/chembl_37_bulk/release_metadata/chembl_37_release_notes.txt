# Data changes and amendments since the last release:
#######################################################


BindingDB Patent Bioactivity Data (SRD_ID = 37) and  SureChEMBL Patent Bioactivity Data (SRC_ID = 38): 13 intra-source and inter-source duplicate documents were identified. When the number of data points was the same, the earliest entry was retained. Otherwise, the entry with the higher number of bioactivity data points was retained.


The chemical probe flag (stored in MOLECULE_DICTIONARY.CHEMICAL_PROBE) has been updated by extracting chemical probe data from chemicalprobes.org and probes-drug.org on 02/04/2026.


New organism targets:
Legacy organism-based assays (mostly antimicrobial assays) were remapped to specific organism targets resulting in ~ 400 new organism entries in the TARGET_DICTIONARY. Organism targets in ChEMBL fields (ASSAY_ORGANISM, ASSAY_TAX_ID) and dictionaries (TARGET_DICTIONARY, ORGANISM CLASS) were updated in line with the primary ontology (NCBI taxonomy; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/taxonomy). Original deposited assay descriptions and document abstracts continue to reflect the original source information to ensure data FAIRness.


Assay strain annotations:
Legacy organism-based assays (mostly antimicrobial assays) were annotated with ASSAY_STRAIN information where this was present in the assay DESCRIPTION but missing from the ASSAY_STRAIN field. Approximately 7300 assays were updated to increase the structure and FAIRness of organism-based assays.


Preferred names for tissues:
The PREF_NAME for tissue targets were made consistent across the TARGET_DICTIONARY and TISSUE_DICTIONARY (using UBERON names as the primary ontology).


Targeted protein degradation data:
A process to identify and extract targeted protein degradation data has been developed (http://chembl.blogspot.com/2026/01/exploring-targeted-protein-degradation.html). The ~29,000 identified bioactivity data points have been annotated using the new MODALITY flag in the ACTIVITIES table (MODALITY = “Targeted protein degradation”). In addition, the target mapping was updated for ~1,200 legacy targeted protein degradation bioassays (corresponding to ~3,000 bioactivities) as part of our curation effort for TPD data. Feedback is welcome whilst this work continues to undergo refinement. The preferred names for PROTEIN-PROTEIN INTERACTION type targets were standardised where possible using recommended names from UniProt, our primary protein ontology (format: degradation effector PREF_NAME/pathogenic protein PREF_NAME).


Chemical structure curation: 
* merged ~400 tautomer duplicates which were not identified by InChI, but were found via a SMILES tautomer hash
* >3000 BindingDB structures for legacy data have been curated

Drug and clinical candidate data:
In ChEMBL 36, there was a significant change in the number of approved drugs, primarily driven by updates to the WHO Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical (ATC) Classification of Drugs (SRC_ID = 41) and the British National Formulary (BNF) source (SRC_ID = 42). A number of compounds from these sources were downgraded to a MAX_PHASE lower than 4 due to a) BNF no longer being regularly updated and b) the consideration of a revised and more stringent interpretation of what constitutes an approved drug within ATC.
As a result, 169 compounds originally annotated in ChEMBL 36 as non-approved ATC drugs in ChEMBL 36 (i.e., with MAX_PHASE ≤ 3) were updated to MAX_PHASE = 4 for CHEMBL 37 following a manual curation process. A compound was considered approved if there was evidence of regulatory approval in at least one jurisdiction (e.g., FDA, EMA, MHRA, PMDA, or equivalent national regulatory authority). Regulatory approval databases and official product information (e.g., SmPCs, FDA labels) were used as primary sources of evidence. Literature-based evidence alone was not considered sufficient evidence for upgrading without regulatory confirmation.
Differences in ATC MAX_PHASE will still exist between recent ChEMBL releases, as this curation effort is ongoing. In some cases, evidence of approval is difficult to confirm, particularly for compounds that may have been discontinued or replaced by newer, more effective drugs.
Care has also been taken to remove the BNF source for some compounds. Additional curation has been performed on synonyms, hierarchy, and other drug data tables for selected Clinical Candidate Compounds, International Nonproprietary Names (INN) for Pharmaceutical Substances, United States Adopted Names (USAN), ATC and BNF compounds. However, as this is not a full drug data release, it reflects only a small subset of drugs.


STANDARD_TEXT_VALUE:
Frequently used text_value found in ACTIVITIES, ACTIVITY_PROPERTIES and ASSAY_PARAMETERS are now automatically standardised using a dictionary-based approach.


ChEMBL-RDF is now using the latest version of UniChem. Available sources have changed.

# New Database Fields:
ACTIVITIES.MODALITY
Annotates the designed compound modality (e.g., “Targeted Protein Degradation”). Contrasting with the ACTION_TYPE field, a compound captured in the MODALITY field may be active or inactive at a target. Initially, the new ACTIVITIES.MODALITY field will capture the output from the Targeted Protein Degradation annotation process that is described above (MODALITY = "Targeted Protein Degradation").
